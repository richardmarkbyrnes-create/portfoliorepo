WHAT IS MIRO INSIGHTS?

Miro Insights, an acquired AI platform, aggregated qualitative customer data across tools like Gong, Salesforce, and Zendesk to surface actionable voice-of-customer insights tied directly to revenue opportunities and the features they requested.


How & why we built a native customer insights app in Miro

Project walkthrough

problem space

There was a disconnect from
where the work happened.

  • Insights provided a solution for fragmented customer feedback but was disconnected from where the real synthesis happened.

    Synthesis happened visually on the Miro canvas during rituals like roadmapping, backlog grooming, and sprint planning.

The opportunity

Scaling Miro Insights adoption to Miro’s 100+ million user base.

The main issue was that we needed to understand how users actually used customer insights in their existing workflows in Miro boards today where the actual synthesis happened.

Interviewing Miro users to understand this space deeper.

Together with Brittney, we conducted 16 interviews and synthesis observation across three contrasting personas: Researchers, Product Managers, and Sales to give us a deeper understanding of how they used insights in their Miro workflows today.

What we identified

Three breakdown points during board synthesis.


Stripped metadata

  • Moving quotes to sticky notes stripped away user tier, source transcripts, and revenue weight.
  • Collaborators couldn’t verify or trace feedback back to sources.


Conflicting focus

  • Researchers needed qualitative depth: verbatims, sentiment, and video snippets.
  • PMs required quantitative macro weight: aggregated ARR or request frequencies


Static canvas data

  • Canvas stickies severed live ties to source databases (Zendesk, Gong).
  • Boards decayed overnight, decoupling active decisions from evolving feedback.

What we learned about

Product managers, research and sales.

Product managers cared most about the volume of a specific feature mentioned.

Researchers care more about holistic themes and patterns while

Sales team cared more about using company data and requests to roadmap their pipelines.

WE GOT TO ideating

We saw potential in this space

We knew how they worked, we recognized the issues and gaps in their existing workflows and we saw room to improve. We ideated and brainstormed potential solutions to that we felt were a viable first step solution to address this problem space.

DESIGN pillars

Trust - Trust behind the data was a huge barrier for adoption

Collaboration - Insights gain value only when they can be seamlessly used in multiplayer environments.

Synced - Data should be bi-directional and synced to avoid data decay and outdated artifacts left on boards.

we landed on 3 potentials routes (3 horizontal rows here)

1. Concept of adding an invisible data layer to already existing features (sticky notes, docs, screenshots) and when selected users can triggers Miro AI to learn more about this specific insight.

2. A brand new insights format that when data was added to inside the format container it automatically sync to our insights database.

3. Structured insight cards components and a new app experience to find and discover feedback straight from the canvas. Winner winner chicken dinner.

WHAT WE BUILT

Turning scattered insight data into structured, multiplayer components on the Miro canvas.

Insight cards bring the source of truth onto the canvas. They restructure themselves depending on where you drop them — compact inside a table, spanning a kanban column, opening out to the full customer quote when they stand alone — so they fit whatever structure a team already works in. Either way the card links back to the original feedback, so anyone can trace a workshop conclusion back to something a customer actually said.

CONTEXT

Stickers are one of Miro's most used features. Around 3.5 million stickers are added to boards every week and have stamped itself as a user favourite for expression while collaborating on the canvas.

Balance delight with organic viral loops and high engagement (retention and collaboration lift).

the demand

We saw lot's of customer requests for more sticker variants, the current options felt limited and stale. Roughly 2,500 requests in 2025 alone including some larger F100 customers. While the demand and user value was clear, it was unsustainable for our team to maintain and grow this internally.
We needed a more scalable and flexible solution.....

THE PROPOSAL

Our team saw an opportunity to utilize generative AI to take advantage of this space by providing a scalable solution for customers while still leverage cutting edge AI technology. In theory, a fun project for us to build and perfect solution for our user base.... win win, right?

THE BUY IN ISSUE

The issue was we needed leadership buy-in to build this capability, we would need to dedicate resources and costly AI tokens to make this which made it a hard sell with no direct revenue return. We needed to re-angle our approach to build this feature in a way that made sense from a business aspect...

So we looked at potential growth tactics to make this a reality.

tieing this to 2 growth paths

1. Revolving this feature around promoting our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

2. Building in adoption triggers to prompt virality across social media platforms to increase Miro sign ups.

WHAT WE BUILT

We designed a core sticker generator feature built on top of existing stickers panel. We gave users the opportunity to upload their own images and photos to generate personalized stickers straight into their own sticker libraries. This built the core foundation to build our growth layers..

GROWTH LAYER

We designed some post creation experiences for users to promote their newly create sticker packs and save them as assets. This was designed to trigger virality through social media and promote our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

MARKETING

We worked closely with the marketing team to build an in-person sticker printing experience. We designed a tablet native UI for users to print custom stickers at these Canvas 26 events across the world (SF, Tokyo, London and Sydney) which also drove impressive metrics to our social media engagement.

THE OUTCOME

255,239 stickers generated to this date, roughly 19% of all stickers added to the canvas are custom made.

469 posts shared across social media platforms with a total of 2.3 million unique views.

biggest learning

Advocating for prioritization of craft and delight can be sometimes difficult with contrasting business priorities.
This project taught me to think outside the box. Pushing the scope and goals of a project can make what usually get's abandoned to something worthwhile and advantageous to a companies vision.
