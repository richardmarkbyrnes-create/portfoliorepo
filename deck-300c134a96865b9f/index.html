WHAT IS MIRO INSIGHTS?

Miro Insights, an acquired AI platform, aggregated qualitative customer data across tools like Gong, Salesforce, and Zendesk to surface actionable voice-of-customer insights tied directly to revenue opportunities and the features they requested.


How & why we built a native customer insights app in Miro

Project walkthrough

problem space

There was a disconnect from
where the work happened.

  • Insights provided a solution for fragmented customer feedback but was disconnected from where the real synthesis happened.

    Synthesis happened during rituals like roadmapping, backlog grooming, and sprint planning. In order to expand our potential user base we needed to close this disconnect and bring Insights to these rituals which often happened on the Miro canvas.

The opportunity

Scaling Miro Insights adoption to Miro’s 100+ million user base.

The main issue was that we needed to understand how users actually used customer insights in their existing workflows and rituals on Miro boards today,

RESEARCH!

Interviewing Miro users to understand this space deeper.

Together with Brittney, we conducted 16 interviews and synthesis observation across three contrasting personas: Researchers, Product Managers, and Sales to give us a deeper understanding of how they used customer insights in their Miro workflows today.

What we identified

Three breakdown points during board synthesis.


Stripped sources

  • Moving quotes to sticky notes stripped away user tier, source transcripts, and revenue weight.
  • Collaborators couldn’t verify or trace feedback back to sources.


Conflicting focus

  • Researchers needed qualitative depth: verbatims, sentiment, and video snippets.
  • PMs required quantitative macro weight: aggregated ARR or request frequencies


Static canvas data

  • Canvas stickies severed live ties to source databases (Zendesk, Gong).
  • Boards decayed overnight, decoupling active decisions from evolving feedback.

What we learned aboutt

Our core personas…

Product managers used feature requests to decide on what to prioritize in their backlogs.

Researchers cared more about holistic themes and patterns across transcripts.

Sales team cared more about using company data and requests to roadmap their pipelines.

BRAINSTORMING

Finding solutions....

We knew how they worked, we recognized the issues and gaps in their existing workflows and we saw room to improve. We ideated and brainstormed potential solutions to that we felt were a viable first step approach to address this problem space.

DESIGN pillars

Trust - Trust behind the data was a huge obstacle during synthesis

Collaboration - Insights gain value mainly when they can be seamlessly used in multiplayer environments.

Synced - Data should be bi-directional and synced to avoid data decay and outdated artifacts left on boards.

we explored on 3 directions

1. Adding a data layer to already existing features (sticky notes, docs, etc..) where users can trigger
Miro AI to learn more about this specific insight.

2. A brand new insights format that when data was added to inside the format container it would automatically sync to our insights database.

3. Structured insight cards components and a new app experience to find and discover feedback straight from the canvas. Winner winner!

WHAT WE BUILT

Turning scattered insight data into structured, multiplayer components on the Miro canvas.

These cards acted as a flexible component for users to bring customer insights closer to their workflows.

Focusing on multiplayer support to increase adoption, synced data bidirectionally to avoid data decay and 3 distinct cards types focused on 3 distinct personas.

CONTEXT

Stickers are one of Miro's most used features. We have a library of 200+ premade stickers that users can drop and use on their boards. Around 3.5 million stickers are added to boards every week.

A very simple feature but a world of delight.

the demand

We saw lot's of customer requests for more sticker variants, the current options felt limited and stale. Roughly 2,500 requests in 2025 alone including some from large enterprise customers. While the demand and user value was clear, it was unsustainable for our team to maintain and grow this internally.

We needed a more scalable solution to expand these libraries and keep up with user demands.....

THE opportunity

We could utilize generative AI to take advantage of this space by providing a scalable, flexible solution for the team while still fostering creative expression for users.

In theory, a fun project for us to build and perfect solution for our user base.... win win, right?

THE BLOCKER

We needed leadership buy-in to build this capability, we would need to dedicate resources and use costly AI tokens to make this happen. It was a hard sell with no direct revenue return.

We had to re-angle our approach to build this feature in a way that made sense from a business aspect. So we looked at potential growth tactics to make this a reality....

growth paths

Some sticky 📝 notes later...

1. Revolving this feature around promoting our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

2. Building in adoption triggers to prompt virality loops across social media platforms to increase Miro sign ups to both core product and the event.

3. An in person printing experience to drive virality at the event, suggested from Robyn (marketing)

WHAT WE BUILT

We designed a core sticker generator feature built on top of existing stickers panel. We gave users the opportunity to upload their own images and photos to generate personalized stickers straight into their own sticker libraries.
This built the core foundation to build our growth layers..

GROWTH LAYER

We designed some post creation triggers for users to promote their newly create sticker packs and save them as assets. These were designed to trigger virality through social media and promote our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

MARKETING

We worked closely with the marketing team to build an in-person sticker printing experience. We designed a tablet native UI for users to print custom stickers at these Canvas 26 events across the world (SF, Tokyo, London and Sydney) which also drove impressive metrics to our social media engagement.
