background

Miro Insights, an acquired AI platform, aggregated qualitative customer data across tools like Gong, Salesforce, and Zendesk to surface actionable voice-of-customer insights tied directly to revenue opportunities and the features they requested.

Tight timelines and leadership pressure to get our first Miro native feature built.

How & why we built a native customer insights app in Miro

Project walkthrough

problem space

Feedback was neatly organized, but siloed from everyday planning rituals.

  • Real product synthesis happens collaboratively during sprint planning, backlog grooming, and roadmapping sessions.

    To drive genuine adoption, insights had to live natively on the Miro canvas where teams already work.

The opportunity

Scaling Miro Insights adoption to Miro’s 100+ million user base.

The main issue was that we needed to understand how users actually used customer insights in their existing workflows and rituals on Miro boards today.

RESEARCH!

Interviewing Miro users to understand this space deeper.

Together with Brittney, we conducted 16 interviews across 3 contrasting personas: Researchers, Product Managers, and Sales to give us a deeper understanding of how they used customer insights in their Miro workflows today.

learnings

Three breakdown points during synthesis.


Stripped sources

  • Moving quotes to sticky notes stripped away user tier, source transcripts, and revenue weight.
  • Collaborators couldn’t verify or trace feedback back to sources.


Conflicting focus

  • Researchers needed qualitative depth: verbatims, sentiment, and video snippets.
  • PMs required quantitative macro weight: aggregated ARR or request frequencies.


Static canvas data

  • Canvas stickies severed live ties to source databases (Zendesk, Gong).
  • Boards decayed overnight, decoupling active decisions from evolving feedback.

learnings

Each team listens for different signals


Product Managers: Use customer feedback to validate prioritization decisions.

UX Researchers: Uncover holistic themes and patterns across transcripts.

Sales Teams: De-risk pipeline deals by tracking customer requirements.

BRAINSTORMING

Translating friction points
into initial solutions.

We knew how they worked, we recognized the issues and gaps in their existing workflows and we saw room to improve. We ideated and brainstormed potential solutions that we felt were a viable first step approach to address this problem space.

design non-negotiableS

Trust:
Surface sources so teams never doubt synthesized data.

Multiplayer:
Enable cross-functional collaboration around every insight.

Bi-Directional Sync:
Keep artifacts continuously updated to eliminate data decay.

3 potential directions


01 Contextual Data Layers

Augment existing primitives (sticky notes, docs) with inline Miro AI triggers to inspect data on demand.

02 Auto-Sync Ingestion Containers

Introduce a structured frame that automatically syncs enclosed feedback directly into a central insights database.

03 Structured Cards & Native App (Selected)

Combine modular insight cards with an in-canvas discovery app to deliver end-to-end feedback retrieval.

WHAT WE BUILT

Turning scattered insight data into structured, multiplayer components on the Miro canvas.

Insight cards, a new discovery app and how it all fit together...

CORE FEATURE 1 WALKTHROUGH

Insights cards
3 Types

Insight Cards consolidated our critical customer data into collaborative, multiplayer components.

They empowered core personas to plan, organize, and execute synthesis workflows by enabling real-time, data-backed decisions. Because the cards synced bi-directionally with our database, insights stayed current even as fresh feedback rolled in.

core feature 2 walkthrough

Discovery App

Our new native app served as an insight-discovery engine, enabling users to find and drop rich customer-feedback cards directly onto their canvas to share with the team.

For the MVP, we launched with a simple search and list view across feature requests, customer feedback, and company cards. We later introduced an AI chat interface that made surfacing relevant insights effortless and conversational.

How it came together in real workflows


SCOPE ADJUSTMENTS

Due to technical constraints, we adjusted the final scope by refining the UI and shifting both the sort option and dedicated video playback to V2. We later resolved these limitations and successfully integrated both features in a subsequent release.

THE RESULTS



Became a core staple in our sales playbook as it resonated with prospect Miro customers and their experiences and acted as a nice upsell moment for Miro Insights to fill this gap.

Adoption rate from our existing user base from our standalone product experience. This drove adoption of Miro and also increased our monthly active usage metrics of our platform.

Tested this new feature on previous interviewees and they expressed an overall positive sentiment which provided validation to our design direction.

THE STRUGGLES

Long term, early engagement was promising, but the feature failed to embed into the daily, habitual workflows of our core personas.

Beyond that, the landscape has fundamentally shifted: two years of AI advancements have completely redefined how our target personas work and behave.

what this taught me

Validation Does Not Equal Habit Formation
Rigorous research and positive early validation are not enough on their own. Changing ingrained daily habits is a far higher bar than earning stated user validation.

Right concept, wrong time
Some early concepts were shelved due to immediate technical constraints. The concept was right, but the technical foundation wasn't mature enough to support it.

CONTEXT

Stickers are one of Miro’s most used features, with around 3.5 million added to boards every week. We provide a library of 200+ expressively designed stickers providing low-friction delight that turns functional canvases into shared cultural spaces.

A simple feature packed with delight.

the demand

We saw lots of customer requests for more sticker variants. Roughly 2,500 requests in 2025 alone. While the demand and user value was clear, it was unsustainable for our team to maintain and grow this internally.

We needed a more scalable solution to keep up with user demands....

THE opportunity

We could utilize generative AI to take advantage of this space by providing a scalable, flexible solution for the team while still fostering creative expression for users.

In theory, a fun project for us to build and a perfect
solution for our user base.... win win, right?

THE BLOCKER

We needed leadership buy-in to build this capability, we would need to dedicate resources and use costly AI tokens to make this happen. It was a hard sell with no direct revenue return.

We had to re-angle our approach to build this feature in a way that made sense from a business aspect. So we looked at potential growth tactics to make this a reality....

growth paths

Many sticky notes and discussions later...

1. Revolving this feature around promoting our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

2. Building in adoption triggers to prompt virality loops across social media platforms to increase Miro sign ups to both core product and the event.

3. An in-person printing experience to drive virality at the event, suggested by Robyn (marketing).

WHAT WE BUILT

We designed a core sticker generator feature built on top of the existing stickers panel. We gave users the opportunity to upload their own images and photos to generate personalized stickers.

GROWTH LAYER

We designed some post creation triggers for users to promote their newly created sticker packs and save them as assets. These were designed to trigger virality loops through social media engagement and promote our upcoming Canvas 26 event.

MARKETING

We worked closely with the marketing team to build an in-person sticker printing experience. We vibe-coded a tablet native UI for users to print custom stickers at these Canvas 26 events across the world (SF, Tokyo, London and Sydney).

THE NUMBERS





Stickers generated to this date, roughly 19% of all stickers added to the canvas are custom.

Million unique views across 469 posts shared across social media platforms.

Paid new Miro account sign ups coming from this campaign.

learning

Thinking outside the box

Advocating for craft and delight often means navigating conflicting business priorities. This project pushed me to think beyond standard constraints, proving that expanding a project's creative ambitions can turn dismissed ideas into features that serve the broader company vision.

what resonated

This feature resonated deeply across different markets, with Japan leading the way. Following overwhelming interest at our Tokyo event, our CSM reported that Japanese users heavily requested to make physical sticker printing a permanent paid feature.

Reading The Culture Map has really underscored this experience, making me reflect more deeply on how cultural nuances drive user perception and engagement.
