Canvas Insights
Turning unstructured feedback into spatial, actionable workflows.

15 min

Role and team

Richard Designer

Kosta Product

Mor, Ophir, Frak Engineers

Brittney UX researcher

The problem

Disconnected workflows

  • Rich customer feedback sat in siloed tools — Zendesk, Gong, Jira, etc.. we created a solution for this but the synthesis that used it happened visually on the canvas, in roadmapping, backlog grooming and sprint planning across product, design, research and even go to market teams.

Research

What the rituals actually needed.

Interviews with product managers, UX researchers and engineering leads, plus workflow mapping of the synthesis rituals themselves.

  • Density vs utility — raw database views are too rigid for ideation; a plain sticky has no actionable metadata and no traceability.
  • Diverse consumption — a PM needs priority metrics and revenue impact; a designer needs the quote and the sentiment.
  • Fluidity over finality — data shouldn’t just sit on the board. It has to move between synthesis, prioritisation and roadmapping.

Design principles

Three rules everything answered to.

  • Context-preserving flexibility — let the data morph without breaking its relational source of truth.
  • Spatial affordance — treat insights as first-class, tangible canvas objects, not decoration.
  • Progressive disclosure — headline signals first, deep customer telemetry on demand.

Ideation

Three ways in, one that held.

  • A — embed the table — familiar, rigid, and it actively fought the spatial freedom of the canvas.
  • B — a passive overlay — low clutter, but no multi-user grouping and nothing to sort.
  • C — a contextual side panel and dynamic smart cards — query in a focused drawer, then drag items out to instantiate as modular, interactive canvas cards.

The trade-off

Data integrity against user autonomy.

  • A read-only live sync would have kept the source of truth perfect, and made the cards useless — you couldn’t regroup or annotate them in the middle of a workshop.
  • We chose autonomy: free-form editing and regrouping on the canvas, deliberately unable to corrupt the upstream database. The board is a workspace, not a window.

The workflow

Query, then instantiate.

  • Navigate and filter in the side panel — across Backlog, Feedback and Company segments.
  • Drag an item onto the canvas and structured feedback becomes a spatial card, with its telemetry mapped in and its link home intact.

Data morphing

One card, three surfaces.

  • Synthesis — sentiment, core quotes and tags, for thematic clustering.
  • Prioritisation — the surface morphs to lead with request volume, ARR weighting and company logos.
  • Execution — one click converts it into a trackable Jira or backlog item with a status link.

System states

The interesting part is the edges.

  • Truncated long-form verbatims, multi-company attribution, missing metadata — each one a state the card has to hold without looking broken or, worse, looking complete when it isn’t.
  • A component that changes shape three ways has to be resilient in all three. Most of the design work lived here rather than in the happy path.

The roadblock

A hundred cards on a live board.

  • Importing 100+ feedback cards at once during a workshop degraded performance and lagged sync — precisely the moment it had to feel effortless.
  • With engineering we set lazy-loading thresholds and lightweight card-rendering heuristics, and I argued for local client-side responsiveness ahead of immediate remote sync to keep multiplayer fluid.

The impact

$0 to $250k ARR.

  • A core driver of the Miro Insights suite scaling from nothing to $250k ARR, and rapid adoption across cross-functional teams running canvas-based prioritisation.
  • Workshop prep and synthesis cycle time both fell significantly — the hours of manual assembly before a session simply stopped being necessary.

Customer feedback

“This helps us make decisions based on data we can trust, and gives stakeholders visibility into results without constant back and forth among teams.”

Retrospective

What V2 would do.

  • Bidirectional write-back. An update made on a canvas card should refresh the upstream feedback platform on its own, with no manual sync trigger.
  • The same shape applies to creator tooling: a product catalogue that morphs cleanly between quick live-stream tagging, high-density dashboard curation, and the viewer-facing shelf.

Project 2 · Miro · 2026

Custom Stickers
Unblocking personal expression
with generative AI.

15 min

The problem

A finite library,
infinite in-jokes.

Miro’s emoji and static sticker set was popular but bounded. In retros and sprints, teams kept running out of ways to react to a specific idea, an inside joke, or anything too niche for a stock icon. Keeping it stocked was our job, and it didn’t scale — every addition was more design and production work for a library that could never cover everything.

What we built

Type it, and it exists.

A text-to-sticker generator inside the panel people already use. Type “taco wearing sunglasses” and get transparent, on-brand options in seconds — the interface applies the outlines and sticker styling behind the scenes, so a few words are enough to get a usable result.

Cross-platform mechanics

One canvas,
three input models.

  • The same panel works under a mouse, a trackpad and a finger. Pointer gives you hover and precision; touch gives you neither, and loses the bottom of the screen to a keyboard the moment the prompt field is focused.
  • [ Your specific decisions — hit targets, drag-to-place vs tap-to-place, what the panel does on tablet. ]

Asset lifecycle

A sticker doesn’t end when it’s generated.

  • Prompt → generation → transparent asset → placed on canvas → copied, resized and reused by everyone else on the board. Only the first two steps are AI; the rest is asset management, and that’s where it scales or falls over.
  • Every generated sticker becomes a real object with a real cost — stored, rendered at every zoom level, and duplicated by people who had nothing to do with making it.

System scalability

Generated content still has to obey the design system.

  • The model can produce anything, which is the point and also the risk. Constraining it to Miro’s sticker language — bold outlines, consistent weight, transparent ground — is what keeps a board from looking like a clip-art folder.
  • Those parameters are applied by the interface, not asked of the user. Someone typing three words still gets something that belongs in the product.

Takeaway

Lowering the barrier to creation gave teams a way to build their own visual language in real time. It raised session energy and interaction counts — proof that generative AI can earn its place through connection, not only productivity.

Bringing it together

Two projects,
one argument for Shopping.

5 min

Speed

Slow now, or slower later.

Canvas Insights worked because we settled what a card was — a pointer, not a copy — before designing a pixel. Stickers worked because the asset lifecycle was decided before the feature scaled. Shopping surfaces move fast and get retrofitted constantly; the cheap moment to decide is always earlier than it feels.

Cohesion

Learn their workflow,
move together faster.

One card had to serve researchers and PMs who wanted opposite things. One sticker panel had to serve a mouse, a trackpad and a finger. Shopping sits between creators, merchants and viewers — three groups who share a surface and share almost nothing else.

Quality

Proper systems build quality outputs.

Both features put a model in front of customers, and in both the trust came from the structure underneath — traceable records in one, enforced sticker language in the other. Shopping is commerce inside content: the surface has to feel effortless precisely because the data behind it isn’t.
