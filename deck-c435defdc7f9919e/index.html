What Insights is

An AI customer feedback aggregator. Millions of data points, thousands of signals.

Sales calls, app reviews, CRM entries, support tickets across multiple integration sources — ingested and sorted automatically into thematic clusters, product areas and opportunity maps.

The journey

How we built, maintained and contributed to Miro’s design system and journey.

The steps it took to get there…

My role

Two jobs, pulling opposite ways.

  • Move Insights into the Miro Design System — so a product that arrived by acquisition could carry a multi-product growth identity rather than its own.
  • Keep shipping features the whole time — customer and leadership feature requests didn’t slow. We needed to keep building.
  • And make it true in two places at once — Insights had to hold up as a standalone tool and as a native part of the canvas: two different code bases with the same user experience.

Before

Scattered Figma files,
growing vision, no system.

Classic early-stage debt. Every new feature request turned into friction for engineering and design, because components started from scratch.

The audit

Many components,
no system.

Failing accessibility requirements, visual inconsistencies with no documentation, massively misaligned with Miro design system and language harming brand reputation and standards.

The trade-off

Adopt where I could.
Bridge where I couldn’t.

  • Matching components with their MDS counterparts, no local variant, no reason to keep our own. If it fit the use case it was updated.
  • Some data-heavy pieces had no counterparts — aggregation tables, dense filters. MDS didn’t support that yet so we kept core components local but updated the tokens.
  • So I built an intermediate library — a deliberate Frankenstein, holding the product’s needs now against the platform’s direction later so we could have a system behind the madness.

From chaos to structure.

Finding structure in the messy middle…

The payoff

Proper structure fuels efficiency.

My designing got faster, more consistent and easier to maintain as we built more features. Quality and maintenance became a breeze.

But one big thing was holding the value back…

Then reality

Immaculate in Figma.
Unshipped in production.

Design files polished, tokens standardised, components documented. Meanwhile engineering was deep in migrating to Miro’s infra and shipping new features — so token colours, button variants and icons sank to the bottom of the backlog.

Learning time

I wanted to push the craft forward
and support where I could.

  • Good timing, we had an offsite in Austin, TX the following week — sat with my engineers and paired with them to learn about how they push changes to our repo.
  • Set up the tooling — Design System MCP, and Claude Code to open pull requests directly using existing MDS components instead of custom made.

The shift

Becoming comfortable with code.
Not so scary.

  • Shipped my first design PR that week — twenty more followed, every one through engineering review and became more comfortable in understanding how design systems work.
  • It turned the smallest, most-deferred class of work into something that no longer needed anyone else’s sprint. Allowed me to push the design system and craft to the next level while strengthening my relationship with my engineering team.

What it taught me

Being comfortable with not knowing.

It’s good to embrace the scary. I started this team being too scared to even look at a GitHub repo.

I ended with a deeper understanding into how Figma component systems become design systems in production, and how it all contributes to a faster and more cohesive experience for design, engineering and the end users.

Design systems
never end.

Maintaining and contributing to a design system never ends. It requires contribution and expansion as a product’s vision grows and shifts.

As we grew as a team and product, so did our needs…

Scoped expansion

Our product offerings grew,
and so did our design needs.

As we started to look beyond features that existed in our own product, we wanted to bring our value to the Miro canvas.

The issue was bringing custom features to the canvas — we had design needs that weren’t possible with the current design system’s components, but we had a need.

Cross organisational

Our engineering team built a scrappy MVP, something that worked, but didn’t scale.

Miro’s system had rigour and standards we needed to align to. Through sharing our vision early and often we got buy-in from several other platform teams — Diagramming, Tables, Roadmapping and more.

They saw utility in our design proposal, and we created a case to bring forward to Eddie, our design systems lead.

Contribution

Working with Eddie
to expand the design system.

We set out on expanding the current utility of our card components. I sat down with the platform teams — Diagramming, Tables, Roadmapping — to learn about their design needs for cards. We needed sub-headers, more room for different sizing options, and more flexibility in the colour values used in tags.

So we built it. Expanded component and boolean properties in Figma and in our design system, and enabled the expansion of our system through collective need.

Retrospective

What I learned from Eddie
and enterprise design systems.

  • Ruthlessly protect what gets prioritised and what doesn’t. Only expanding design systems and functionality once there is a collective need.
  • Clear documentation and accessibility for engineering, design and more.

Beyond the product

Pushing brand consistency
to our web presence.

I had the opportunity to build and maintain our team’s website and help centre, all built in Framer using the exact design language and properties from our Figma and brand centre.

Driving consistency and craft across product and web.

Cohesion across domains

One visual language,
a lot of people relying on it.

The visibility and importance proper system guidelines for brand and visual language have for so many different cross-functional partners.

Thank you

Shoutout to everyone involved.

From the Insights team, design systems, marketing and our platform teams — they all pushed my understanding and appreciation for consistent, systemic design further.

Retrospective

What I learned from all of this…
Speed, Cohesion and Quality.

Speed

Slow now, or slower later.

Taking a systematic and structured approach to product development can be time consuming and sometimes painful. One thing I know now: it’s nowhere near as painful as trying to fix the lack of systems later.

Cohesion

Learn their workflow,
move together faster.

Applying an empathic mindset to cross-functional partners can move everyone together faster. Learning their needs and their workflows can amplify your team’s progress, efficiency and relationships tenfold.

Quality

Proper systems build quality outputs.

Structure across processes eventually bleeds out into the consumer-facing experience. Doing things right leads to long-term quality and consistency.
