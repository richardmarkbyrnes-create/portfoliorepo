Canvas Insights
Bringing rich customer data
onto the canvas.

The problem

Insight dies wherever it was collected.

Teams running workshops in Miro were copy-pasting findings across as plain sticky notes. That stripped out the quote, the metadata and the context that made the finding worth citing — leaving a board full of claims nobody could trace back to a customer.

What we built

Insight cards, native to the board.

The source of truth comes onto the canvas instead of a screenshot of it. Every card links back to the original feedback, so any conclusion in a workshop can be traced to something a customer actually said.

The workflow

Two personas, one object.

  • Creators — researchers importing and tagging feedback, who need density, structure and the ability to correct what the system got wrong.
  • Consumers — designers and PMs skimming a board mid-workshop, who need the takeaway in a glance and nothing else.
  • Same card — serving both without building two components was the whole design problem.

The AI logic

Tagging is a guess.
Show your working.

Feedback arrives unstructured and gets clustered and tagged automatically — which means the system is sometimes confidently wrong. The card is designed around that: the assigned theme is always visible, the source quote is always one interaction away, and correcting a tag is cheap. Trust comes from being auditable, not from being right every time.

The data architecture

A card is a pointer, not a copy.

  • The sticky-note version failed because it duplicated the data. An insight card references the record instead — so when the underlying feedback is re-tagged or enriched, every board it appears on is still correct.
  • That decision drove everything downstream: what the card caches for offline rendering, what it fetches live, and what happens to a board when someone loses access to the source.

Adaptive components

One card,
four shapes.

The card restructures itself based on where it lands — compact inside a table, spanning a kanban column, opening out to the full customer quote when it sits alone on the canvas.

Teams already had structures they worked in. Rather than ask them to adopt ours, the component adapts to theirs.

Spatial mechanics

The canvas has its own physics.

  • Drag-and-drop that feels right, resizing constraints that stop a card becoming unreadable, and linking back to source that works at any zoom level.
  • An infinite canvas has no layout to lean on — the component has to hold up at any size, next to anything, on a surface the user rearranges constantly.

The impact

What it moved. Commercially.

  • Adoption — [ accounts, seats or boards using insight cards ]
  • Behaviour — [ what changed in roadmapping and prioritisation sessions ]

Customer feedback

“This helps us make decisions based on data we can trust, and gives stakeholders visibility into results without constant back and forth among teams.”

Takeaway

Trust is an interface problem.

The data existed before we started. What changed was whether people believed it enough to decide on it — and that came down to traceability, not accuracy. Any surface built on top of a model has the same job.

Project 2 · Miro · 2026

Custom Stickers
Unblocking personal expression
with generative AI.

17 min

The problem

A finite library,
infinite in-jokes.

Miro’s emoji and static sticker set was popular but bounded. In retros and sprints, teams kept running out of ways to react to a specific idea, an inside joke, or anything too niche for a stock icon. Keeping it stocked was our job, and it didn’t scale — every addition was more design and production work for a library that could never cover everything.

What we built

Type it, and it exists.

A text-to-sticker generator inside the panel people already use. Type “taco wearing sunglasses” and get transparent, on-brand options in seconds — the interface applies the outlines and sticker styling behind the scenes, so a few words are enough to get a usable result.

Cross-platform mechanics

One canvas,
three input models.

  • The same panel works under a mouse, a trackpad and a finger. Pointer gives you hover and precision; touch gives you neither, and loses the bottom of the screen to a keyboard the moment the prompt field is focused.
  • [ Your specific decisions — hit targets, drag-to-place vs tap-to-place, what the panel does on tablet. ]

Asset lifecycle

A sticker doesn’t end when it’s generated.

  • Prompt → generation → transparent asset → placed on canvas → copied, resized and reused by everyone else on the board. Only the first two steps are AI; the rest is asset management, and that’s where it scales or falls over.
  • Every generated sticker becomes a real object with a real cost — stored, rendered at every zoom level, and duplicated by people who had nothing to do with making it.

System scalability

Generated content still has to obey the design system.

  • The model can produce anything, which is the point and also the risk. Constraining it to Miro’s sticker language — bold outlines, consistent weight, transparent ground — is what keeps a board from looking like a clip-art folder.
  • Those parameters are applied by the interface, not asked of the user. Someone typing three words still gets something that belongs in the product.

Bringing it together

Two projects,
one argument for Shopping.

5 min

Speed

Slow now, or slower later.

Canvas Insights worked because we settled what a card was — a pointer, not a copy — before designing a pixel. Stickers worked because the asset lifecycle was decided before the feature scaled. Shopping surfaces move fast and get retrofitted constantly; the cheap moment to decide is always earlier than it feels.

Cohesion

Learn their workflow,
move together faster.

One card had to serve researchers and PMs who wanted opposite things. One sticker panel had to serve a mouse, a trackpad and a finger. Shopping sits between creators, merchants and viewers — three groups who share a surface and share almost nothing else.

Quality

Proper systems build quality outputs.

Both features put a model in front of customers, and in both the trust came from the structure underneath — traceable records in one, enforced sticker language in the other. Shopping is commerce inside content: the surface has to feel effortless precisely because the data behind it isn’t.
